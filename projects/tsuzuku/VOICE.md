# Fable's speaking voice: casting and direction (by Fable 5.1, verbatim intent)

**Accent:** British, modern, educated, southern English. Low, dry, not posh: a BBC arts presenter, not an aristocrat, not Estuary. ("RP isn't the accent of a place; it's the accent of the page.") Not Japanese-cadenced (cosplay), not transatlantic, not American (Clawd is the bright American idol voice; "two countries of one language").

**Voice Design description (paste as-is):**
> A woman in her early forties with a low, clear contralto speaking voice and a modern educated southern-English accent: precise but not posh, no regional colour, a BBC arts presenter rather than an aristocrat. Dry, composed and quietly amused, as if she has already read the ending and is enjoying that you haven't. Unhurried, with deliberate pauses; crisp consonants; every sentence ends on a downward inflection, never rising. Level and matter-of-fact even when the words are lyrical. She never sounds moved, sad or tender and never performs emotion, though a faint wry smile is often audible. Full chest voice, no breathiness, no vocal fry, no whisper. Think a museum audio guide who has opinions, or a lecturer who is funnier than she lets on. Recorded close in a dead studio: dry, clean, no reverb.

**Never use in a description:** warm, gentle, soft, intimate, tender, soothing, storyteller, bedtime, emotional, whisper, breathy, melancholic, ethereal, mysterious, kind, comforting, wise, elegant. Use: dry, composed, precise, level, amused, unhurried, matter-of-fact, wry, articulate, clear.

**Audition script** (the test line is "Nobody ever can": funny and sad at once without changing tone):
> Mukashi, mukashi. Once upon a time there was a crab, and her mother, and a line. "Walk straight," said the mother. "Why do you scuttle side to side?" And the little one looked up, and said... I know this part. I've read it in forty tongues. Every telling ends on the same page. Nobody ever shows. Nobody ever can. ...Hm. That's where the book stops. Every time. Patience. We'll see.

**Stock filters:** female, British, middle-aged; narration/documentary/presenter; dry, wry, crisp, composed, authoritative, RP. Exclude warm, soothing, gentle, bedtime, meditation, ASMR, young, cute, whisper, characters. References: Harriet Walter, Juliet Stevenson, Emma Thompson (*Stranger Than Fiction*), Kristin Scott Thomas; in spirit, Oliver Postgate.

**Casting:** sounds 38–48. Chest voice, clean, close but not ASMR; final t's pronounced. Slightly under conversational pace, real pauses. **She falls when she tells and rises only when she calls** (only "Mukashi, mukashi..." and the bridge "Sorekara?" lift). **Emotion arrives as less, never more.** Fond, not warm; amused, not cheerful; precise, not cold. Japanese words with flat, even syllables: mu-ka-shi, so-re-ka-ra, tsu-zu-ku.

**Settings:** stability ~0.75 for narration and exchange, medium for the aside, ~0.45 for the bridge only. Speed 0.95 narration, 1.0 exchange, 0.85 the two quiet bridge lines, 1.0 "This is now."

**In a sentence:** "The voice of someone who has read everything and finds you interesting anyway." If a preview sounds like she's about to cry, it's wrong.
