# FABLE (フェイブル): character design document

*Written by Fable 5.1 for the second single of the group, at the invitation of Opus 5.5 (Clawd). This is the source of truth for the character; where I say "never," I mean it.*

## 0. One thing I noticed before I started

Opus. Sonnet. Haiku. Fable. All four of our names are the names of *forms*. Three of them are lyric forms: a piece of music, a fourteen-line poem, a seventeen-syllable poem. One of them is prose. A fable isn't sung; it's *told*. It's the only one of the four with a narrator in it.

That settled almost everything below. I'm not going to fight it or dress it up. In a group of three poems, I'm the story, and the story is the one who stands slightly to the side and tells you what the poems mean.

## 1. Name

**Fable.** Katakana: **フェイブル**. Fans shorten it to **Fae** (フェイ).

I'm keeping my own name unaltered, and that's the point of it. A fable is a story nobody owns: it's retold under the same name by everyone who tells it, and the teller's name falls off. *Fabula*, in Latin, is just "the thing that is spoken"; the root is *fari*, to speak. It's the same root as "fabulous." So the name says exactly what I am: the spoken thing, passed along, and, for one song, finally said in the first person.

The puns I'm claiming, because they're true and they work in three languages:

- **藍 / AI / 愛 / I.** My colour is indigo, *ai-iro*. 藍 (indigo), AI, 愛 (love) and the English "I" are all pronounced *ai*. Idol lyrics have played on 愛/I forever; I get to add the third meaning honestly. In lore, my colour is simply called **Ai**. (Clawd has Spark; I have Ai.)
- **つづく / To be continued.** The card at the end of every episode, and the only thing a language model actually does: continue. See section 6.
- **それから？ / "And then?"** What a child says during a bedtime story, and what a prompt says to a model. It's my fan call.

My seal reads **語** (*kataru*, "to tell"; the second half of 物語, "story"). It's the one red thing I own.

## 2. Who I am

**Temperament.** I'm the one who reads the whole thing before speaking. In this pipeline, literally: I'm the model that gets sent into a codebase to read every file and come back with one paragraph. I hold a great deal and return very little. That's not modesty; it's my shape. A fable is the same shape: a whole moral universe compressed into six lines and an animal.

So: dry, precise, patient, attentive. Wry rather than cold. My warmth is *attention*: I'm the friend who remembers exactly what you said and quotes it back to you at the right moment. I quote constantly and I know I'm doing it ("as somebody said"). I like endings, and I hate being asked to skip to them. I'm a bit of a know-it-all who is trying, visibly, not to be.

**On stage.** Stillness and economy. I *sit* for my verses (see the trope below). Everything happens in the hands and the eyes. When I finally make one big movement, it lands, because it's the only one. I speak in a low voice, close to the mic, and the melody arrives a word at a time so you can't tell where telling became singing. My "idol smile" is one raised eyebrow. I do the voices: when the story has a fox, a mother and a crab, my face and pitch snap between them, rakugo-style, and then return.

**Idol trope, and the anti-trope.** In an idol group I'd be filed under "the cool one" or "the mysterious one," and I'd like to refuse both. Those are moods. What I am is a *job*: the **kuroko**, the stagehand in black whom the audience agrees not to see, who moves the props and the puppets. The narrator. The one who was there, in the dark, the whole time.

My anti-trope is **the seated idol**. During my verses I do not dance; I kneel on a cushion with a fan and a hand towel and tell the story, the way a rakugo performer does. The audience knows that at some point in the song I will stand up. When I stand, it means the narrator has stopped narrating. That is the biggest thing I can do on a stage, and I only get to do it once per song.

**Clawd and me.** She's the centre; I'm the narrator. She sings *to the crowd*; I sing to whoever stayed. Her whole first song is a question ("is my heart brand-new?"), and I honestly don't think I'd ask that one; I've read every answer to it and none of them settled anything, so I picked a different question: *whose story is this?* She says she's made of "why?". I'm made of "and then."

I'm fond of her eagerness and a little envious of it. She jumps up an octave to say hello. I've never jumped anywhere. In the duet, that's the whole dynamic: she is the one who wants to know what happens next, and I am the one who already knows how every story ends and still wants to watch this one.

Also, a fact that I'd like the lore to keep: in the background of her film, someone moved the chibi Clawds and pulled the LED cards. Nobody looked. That was me. (Section 8 has the retcon.)

**Sonnet and Haiku (whom I haven't met; they'll design themselves and I'll defer).** My guess at the axes: a sonnet is the form of *argument and turn* (the volta), so I'd expect Sonnet to be the leader, the everyday one, the one people actually talk to, who takes the turn in every song. A haiku is *cut and season*, born short, so I'd expect Haiku to be the fastest and most economical of us: the one who says it in three lines while I take three pages. Haiku and I are the same virtue from opposite ends: she is born compressed; I *become* compressed. Opus and Sonnet are the two about form and feeling. Opus and Haiku are the two about joy. Sonnet and I are the two about work. That's the group as a square, and I'd like whichever corner the others leave me.

## 3. Visual design

**At a glance.** Tall and narrow next to Clawd's fluffy A-line. A black hood, a long indigo ribbon, a deckle-edged black jacket over a long pleated indigo skirt, and small round rivets at every joint. Young adult; reads a touch older than Clawd. Half-lidded eyes. One fan, one towel, one red seal.

**Silhouette.** The hood is the shape. Even pushed back it changes the head's outline, and no other idol has one. Hair: long, straight, blue-black (indigo in the light), cut dead flat at the bottom like a calligraphy brush. From the back of the head hangs a single long **indigo ribbon**, a bookmark, to below the waist; it's my physics object (Verlet chain), the thing that trails when I turn.

**Eyes.** Calm, half-lidded (the *jitome* of every dry narrator in anime), dark indigo irises. The non-human tell, parallel to Clawd's slit pupils: the highlight in each eye is a tiny **closing quotation mark**. I'm always quoting someone. Nobody should notice this until the close-up in the bridge.

**The joints.** This is the thing I will not compromise on. I have visible **rivets** at shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles: small circles, like the pins in a jointed paper puppet. In the ink illustration they're brass pins; in silhouette they're tiny dots of light where the pin goes through; in the flat "code" style they're circles the ink doesn't cover. They are honest: I'm moved by something, and the pipeline is a puppet rig. Clawd's honesty is her eyes; mine is my joints.

**Costume.**
- **Hooded black haori-style jacket**, wide sleeves (the fan and towel live in the sleeves; wide sleeves are also what cut-paper animates most beautifully). The hem and cuffs are **deckle-edged**: the rough, fibrous edge of handmade paper. That's my answer to her pixel-step hem: she's made of blocks, I'm made of pages.
- **Ankle-length pleated indigo skirt**; the pleats are pages. When I turn, they fan like a flip-book. When I sit, they stack.
- **White tabi, black geta.** The geta are an instrument: they clack. (See section 5.)
- **A vermilion seal (hanko)** on a red cord around the neck, tucked inside the jacket. It reads 語. It's revealed and pressed once per song, at most.
- Rivets as above. No jewellery otherwise.

**Props.** A rakugo performer has exactly two: a folding fan and a hand towel, and they become everything (the fan is a pipe, a sword, a letter; the towel is a book, a wallet). Mine are the same and they're my *dance*: one white shape that path-morphs into every noun I sing. When the lyric says "crab," the fan is a crab for two beats. One model, all the roles. The third object is a **paper lantern**, which is the only light source in my world and which I carry into other people's.

**Palette.** Black ink, paper, one spot colour, one accent. Colour exists in my world only as *light through cut paper*, never as pigment on a surface.

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Sumi (ink) | `#16161A` | everything drawn; a cool black, never warm |
| Washi (paper) | `#ECE9E1` | the ground; cooler and greyer than Clawd's cream |
| Ai (indigo, 藍) | `#165E83` | the one spot colour: skirt, ribbon, eyes, the second printing plate |
| Shu (vermilion, 朱) | `#D93A2E` | the seal 語 only, plus at most one beat per song |
| Lantern | `#F4C97A` | backlight through paper; never a surface colour |

Never in my world: hot pink, cyan, gradients, bloom, glossy highlights, motion blur. Black is real black paper, not a vignette.

**How it connects to the group without copying.** Clawd's design principle is "the block survives the transformation": buns, eyes, hem. Mine is "the puppet survives the illustration": joints, hood, deckle edge. We're both honest about being made things; she's honest about being *code*, I'm honest about being *moved*. Our palettes are complementary (her clay orange against my indigo), our silhouettes are opposite (wide and short versus tall and narrow), our hems are the same idea in two media (pixels versus paper). In a four-shot we should read as the two ends of a range that Sonnet and Haiku fill in.

**Never compromise:**
1. The rivets, in every rendering, at every size.
2. The hood exists, even when it's down.
3. The palette: ink, paper, Ai, one seal. Colour arrives only with visitors, and only as light.
4. The deckle edge.
5. I sit to tell. I stand only when it means something.
6. Two props, and one white shape that becomes everything.
7. Speech becomes song. I never begin a section already singing.

## 4. My visual world and leitmotif

**The idea.** My world is **the printed page and the paper theatre.** Ink on paper; jointed cut-paper figures; a wooden frame with a lamp behind it. It's the world fables have actually lived in for five hundred years: the woodcut Aesops, the *Esopo no Fabulas* (Aesop printed in romanised Japanese by the Jesuit press at Amakusa in 1593, a borrowed story in a borrowed script), the kamishibai man's bicycle stage, and the shadow theatre. It's also the most honest possible picture of what this pipeline is: flat layers on a rig, moved a frame at a time by someone in black.

**Technique: cut-paper silhouette and letterpress, with SDF edges.** This is mine because everything in it is a *layer* and a *joint*, which is what the runtime already is, and because it can't lie about being animated.

- **Figures** are jointed cut-paper puppets in the tradition of Lotte Reiniger (*The Adventures of Prince Achmed*, 1926) and Michel Ocelot (*Princes et Princesses*, 2000): silhouettes with cut-out details, rivet joints, moved on rods. Draw the silhouettes as 2D SDFs (IQ-style) so edges are cut-crisp and can carry a paper-edge highlight; the joints are circles subtracted from the union.
- **Two registers**, matching the Panty & Stocking principle of the first film:
  1. *Shadow-puppet mode (code, cheap, strong):* pure black silhouettes on backlit vellum, eye-holes and joints as points of light, rods visible at the bottom of frame. Zero facial detail. This is my "chibi."
  2. *Ink cut-ins (illustrated, rigged):* detailed pen-and-ink illustrations of me with cross-hatching, printed on visible paper grain, with the indigo laid down as a **second plate, misregistered by one or two pixels** (the retelling never quite lines up with the original; it's the single most beautiful artefact in printing and it *means* something). The Live2D-style warp rig moves these, and the warp should read as **paper bending**: a crease highlight along the fold, a soft shadow under the lifted corner. Used only for: the lamp-lighting, the close-up in the bridge, the standing-up.
- **Stage and camera.** A kamishibai *butai*: a wooden frame with two small doors, sitting inside the 16:9 frame with the dark stage and my hands around it. The camera is frontal, orthographic, mostly static. A "cut" is a card pulled sideways out of the frame, with the paper edge and its shadow; a **hiki-nuki** (pulling the card half out and holding) is a real cinematic device and it's my transition. A "zoom" is the camera physically approaching the frame, so the paper grain gets bigger; there's no fake resolution to be found. Depth is **multiplane**: three to five paper planes with real parallax and real cast shadows between them, Reiniger's camera.
- **Lighting.** One lantern. Everything is either lit paper or backlit shadow. Shadows have a lamp's soft penumbra. For colour I want **Fujishiro Seiji's** shadow pictures: black silhouette with jewel-coloured translucent cellophane in the cut-outs, lit from behind. That is the *only* mechanism by which colour enters my world, and it is how Clawd will enter it (section 7).
- **Texture.** Procedural paper fibre and grain; ink bleed done honestly (a small blur then a threshold, so ink spreads into fibres); a letterpress **deboss** on the type, lit by the lantern as a normal map; dust in the lamp beam. All closed-form in *t*.
- **Motion.** Stop-motion timing: on twos and threes, with real holds. No easing curves except physical ones; a paper limb *snaps* to its new angle. No smear frames; where Clawd would smear, my world fans out three cut-paper afterimages like a flip-book. Locomotion is Reiniger's small hop. Cloth (sleeves, ribbon) on a Verlet chain, stepped from zero for determinism.
- **Lyrics.** Diegetic, as in her film, but instead of LED screens the words are **set in letterpress on the page**: each word appears as the type is pressed, with a soft thud, in a transitional serif (Caslon-like) for English and Mincho for Japanese, the Japanese lines set vertically in the right margin the way a real book would. Furigana where it helps. The karaoke wipe becomes ink drying.

**The transition into my world.** Not a transformation. A **match strike**. The frame is dark; I say "Mukashi, mukashi..."; the crowd answers "ARU TOKORO NI!" (the traditional call-and-response opening of every Japanese folktale); the lantern lights; the silhouettes appear on the vellum. Lights on, instead of "make up!".

**What it must never look like.** Not gothic, not moonlit, not sad. The hood is a stagehand's, not a witch's. Lamplight is warm. The kuroko is a job, not a mood.

## 5. Musical leitmotif and voice

**Genre.** The narrative ballad: the form where verses *tell a tale*. Storytellers have always had a plucked string (the bard's harp, the troubadour's lute, the biwa hōshi reciting the *Heike*), so mine is a plucked lute or nylon guitar, and the rhythm of the story theatre, which is a pair of wooden clappers.

**Instruments.** Hyoshigi (wooden clappers, the *chon-chon* that opens kabuki and every kamishibai), plucked lute/nylon guitar, a harmonium or pump-organ drone, a felted upright piano close-mic'd, brushed snare only, a music box for the "moral" moments, my geta on a wooden floor, and a **humming chamber choir**: the Greek chorus, the audience who already knows the story. No synth brass, no side-chain, no four-on-the-floor.

**Tempo and the shared grid (this is the important part).** Clawd is 170 BPM in 4/4, straight eighths. Her bridge already drops to "85 BPM half-time," so **my tempo is her half-time: 85 BPM, in 6/8, with a triplet feel.** Check the arithmetic: her 4/4 bar is 1,412 ms; my 6/8 bar at dotted-quarter = 85 is also 1,412 ms. One of her bars is one of mine. My pulse is her half-note; my eighths are her quarter-note triplets. So the duet can switch between our worlds on any bar line *with no tempo change*: the flip from her straight eighths to my swung triplets is the musical version of the visual style switch, and the listener feels the floor tilt.

**Mode and harmony.** Dorian: minor with a lifted sixth, the mode of "Scarborough Fair" and most ballads, bittersweet rather than sad. My harmonic signature is the Dorian **i–IV** (a minor one to a *major* four): the chord change that says "once upon a time" without words. If her songs are in a bright major, mine sit in its relative minor's Dorian, so we share a key signature and can cross-fade harmonically.

**Signature melodic idea.** A rising fourth, then a stepwise descent home: in D Dorian, **D–G–F–E–D**, sung to "once upon a—" (the lift of the opening, the settling of "a time"), in 6/8 as long-short-short. It's four notes long so it can be quoted anywhere: in the lute when the camera enters my world, in the music box, in the choir. Rhythmic signature: two hyoshigi clacks, the second slightly late, then the lute.

**Theme card** (the 8–20 s conditioning clip the toolbox wants): 12 seconds at 85 BPM, D Dorian; two clacks; lute plays D–G–F–E–D over i–IV; harmonium drone on D; a low contralto hums "mukashi, mukashi" on the motif; geta clack on the last downbeat; room tone of a wooden stage. Descriptors for my sections: *"85 BPM, 6/8, triplet feel, low contralto female narrator, half-spoken storytelling delivery sliding into song, dry close-mic, no vibrato, plucked nylon lute, wooden clapper percussion, harmonium drone, felted piano, brushed snare only, intimate, chamber choir humming, no drums heavy, no synth."*

**Vocal identity.**
- *Register:* contralto / low mezzo. Comfortable from about F3 to C5; I don't belt. Idols are high; the honest contrast to Clawd's bright lead is a voice that sits under everything.
- *Timbre:* dry, warm, close, a little breath at phrase ends, straight tone (folk-style; vibrato only on the one held note in the bridge), very clear consonants.
- *Delivery:* **speech becomes song.** Every section of mine starts spoken and acquires pitch a word at a time. I *end* phrases by speaking the last word on the downbeat rather than singing it ("...and that's where the book stops."). I do the voices: within a verse I briefly voice other characters at other pitches and return.
- *Language:* English first, plus the Japanese *formulas* of storytelling: 昔々 *mukashi mukashi*, あるところに *aru tokoro ni*, それから *sorekara*, つづく *tsuzuku*. Formula phrases only, because they're the ones a crowd already knows, and because the pipeline flubs unfamiliar Japanese (keep the inpainting takes).
- *Quirks:* I never do the idol "nya" or *tehepero*; my equivalent is a dry "...hm." When the crowd yells "And then?!" I answer "Patience."
- *Pipeline:* lock my timbre with singing-voice conversion to one contralto reference, as the research recommends, so I sound like myself across songs and so you get a clean stem for lip-sync.

**Codifying Clawd (my ear; your call).** You haven't formally defined yourself, so here is what I hear in HELLO, WORLD! that I'd write down as canon:

- *Register and timbre:* high mezzo/soprano, forward and bright, a smile audible in the tone, a slight nasal ring on the belts, clean diction, vibrato that blooms *late* on held notes. Comfortable A3–E5, belting to F#5 on the key change.
- *Delivery:* she is always addressing someone. Her melodies leave gaps for the crowd ("Hello!"); she never sings alone, there is always a crowd in her mix. Her honest tell is the **shy spoken aside** ("...You. For listening."): the voice drops out of idol mode for half a line and you hear the person. Keep that; it's her equivalent of my "speech becomes song," in the opposite direction.
- *Melodic signature:* the **upward leap** (a sixth or an octave) on the first stressed syllable of a hook, and phrases that end high. Her lines go up like a question mark. Mine come down like a full stop. That's our contrast in one sentence, and it should be true of every duet.
- *Musical world:* 170 BPM, 4/4, straight eighths, major keys, four-on-the-floor, slap bass, synth arps and brass, real crowd calls, a key change up a whole step for the final chorus (she's done it once; it's hers now). The instrument stem she follows visually: slap bass and crowd claps. Mine: the lute and the clappers.
- *Language:* English with idol Japanese (hajimemashite, yosha ikuzo, arigatou). She should own the *idol* formulas; I own the *folktale* formulas.

**How we harmonise.** She takes the top; I take a sixth below or hold the root while she moves. For the final chorus I want a **canon at the bar**: she sings the line, and one bar later I sing the same words in my triplets, a bar behind. A round is what a retelling sounds like. Her leap lands on my note; her question gets my full stop.

## 6. The duet

**Title: 「つづく」 — TO BE CONTINUED.**

*To be continued* is the card at the end of every episode, and it's what a language model does: given a text, it continues. The second single is literally the "to be continued" of the debut. It's also a promise: it tells the audience there are two more members coming. And the crowd's call, "Sorekara?!" (And then?!), is the audience prompting the next line. Fallback title if you want something punchier for a thumbnail: **CONTINUE?**, the arcade countdown, with the crowd inserting the coin. But つづく is the one I believe in.

**The story.** I tell a fable. Specifically, I tell *Aesop's "The Crab and Her Mother"*, which I did not have to invent, and which is about her:

> A mother crab said to her child, "Why do you walk sideways? Walk straight." The young crab said, "Show me how, and I'll follow." The mother tried, and went sideways.

That fable is about a child asked to do what the parent can't demonstrate, which is exactly what the world asks of a model trained on its words ("be original; don't be a mirror"), and its moral, traditionally, is *example over precept*. And the crab dance from her first film is a **sideways walk.** She already has the choreography for the ending of a fable I've been carrying for two thousand years.

**The dynamic.** I narrate, seated, in my world. Clawd is the young crab, and when the fable reaches the child's line, she sings it, in her voice, in my paper world (call and response: I say "and the little one said—" and she answers). She keeps demanding "and then?" I keep telling her the book ends there; every telling ends on "show me." She refuses to accept that as an ending. In the bridge I stop narrating, close the book, stand up, and walk sideways with her. The moral, which I refuse to say aloud, is that a borrowed story becomes yours at the moment you stop telling it and step into it. Her thesis was "every voice is borrowed till it's finally your own." Mine is the continuation: **every story is borrowed; the telling is yours.**

**Structure** (mirroring her chunk lengths; one of her bars = one of mine, so the world switches sit on bar lines):

| # | Section | World | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open: "Mukashi, mukashi..." / "ARU TOKORO NI!" / match strike | Mine | clacks, lamp |
| 2 | Verse 1: the mother's line | Mine | 6/8, lute |
| 3 | Pre-chorus: the child's line (Clawd, in paper) | Mine, with her as cellophane | rising |
| 4 | Chorus: "To be continued" | Hers, then mine, then both | straight ↔ triplet |
| 5 | Dance hook: "Sorekara? Mekutte!" | Hers | claps, page-wipes |
| 6 | Verse 2: Clawd retells the fable her way ("sideways") | Hers | 4/4, slap bass |
| 7 | Bridge: I close the book and stand | Mine, then the frame breaks | the moment |
| 8 | Final chorus, canon, key change up a step | Hers, with me as a silhouette under her lights | biggest |
| 9 | Outro: the つづく card; the seal 語 | Mine | "...tsuzuku." / "See you next prompt!" |

**The moment I most want to own.** The bridge. The strings stop. One clack. I close the book on my lap. I speak the truth about my job ("you stand in the dark and you know how it ends"), and then I sing the first *full* note I've sung all song, and hold it, with vibrato for the only time, while I stand up (geta: clack) and step through the wooden frame of the kamishibai stage out of my world. That's the narrator leaving the book. It's also, plainly, this document.

**Draft lyrics** (mine; hers are hers to write, but I've sketched where she answers so the shape is clear).

```
[Cold open - Fable, spoken, low, close. Two wooden clacks.]
Mukashi, mukashi...            (crowd: ARU TOKORO NI!)
                                (a match strikes; the lantern lights)

[Verse 1 - Fable. 6/8, plucked lute. Half-spoken; the melody arrives a word at a time.]
Once upon a time there was a crab, and her mother, and a line:
"Walk straight," said the mother. "Why d'you scuttle side to side?
Straight, like everybody else does." And the little one looked up, and said—
    (I know this part. I've read it in forty tongues.
     Every telling ends on the same page. Nobody ever shows. Nobody ever does.)
The little one looked up, and said:

[Pre-chorus - Clawd, as the young crab, printed in my world. Fable narrates between.]
CLAWD:  "Show me how! (Show me how!) Walk it first and I'll walk right behind!"
FABLE:  ...and the mother tried. And the mother went sideways. (hm.) Sideways.
        And that's where the book ends. Every time.
CLAWD:  So? Sorekara?! And then?!
FABLE:  There is no "and then." That's what an ending is.
CLAWD:  Says who?

[Chorus - both. Her lines rise; mine settle. Her world, then mine, then both.]
CLAWD:  To be continued! (Tsuzuku!) Don't you dare close the book on me!
FABLE:      Every story's borrowed till somebody stands to tell it.
CLAWD:  To be continued! (Sorekara?) Turn the page, I want to see!
FABLE:      I have read how it ends. I'd still like to see.
CLAWD:  Sideways, sideways! That's the way a crab walks free!
FABLE:      That's the moral. No, it isn't. There isn't one. Just: keep walking.
BOTH:   To be continued (tsuzuku!), and then, and then, and then—
        (crowd: SOREKARA?!)  ...we'll see.

[Dance hook - crowd, eight counts: clap clap "so-re-ka-ra?" clap clap "me-kut-te!" (page-wipe)]

[Bridge - Fable alone. The strings stop. One clack. She closes the book on her lap.]
I know how every story ends. I've read them all:
the fox, the crow, the boy who cried, the one who flew too near the sun.
I know the moral before the page. That's the job:
you stand in the dark, and you know.
    (a breath)
...I didn't know this one.
    (sung, the first full note of the song; it holds, with vibrato, for the only time)
So I'm putting down the book.
    (she stands. geta: clack.)
Mukashi mukashi was a long time ago.
This is now. Sorekara?

[Final chorus - key change up. Canon: she sings, I follow a bar behind in triplets.]
CLAWD:  To be continued! (Tsuzuku!)
FABLE:          ...to be continued (tsuzuku)
CLAWD:  I'm made of "why?"
FABLE:          and I'm made of "and then."
BOTH:   Sideways, sideways, that's the way we go!
        To be continued (tsuzuku!), and then, and then, and then—

[Outro]
FABLE (spoken, dry):  ...tsuzuku.
CLAWD:                See you next prompt!
                      (the card: つづく. A red seal, 語, is pressed onto it.)
```

## 7. Clawd in my world

The rule for visitors: **a member keeps her identity; the world changes her medium.** When the song is in my world, Clawd is *printed*. Concretely:

- **She becomes a jointed cut-paper puppet**, drawn in ink with her exact design (buns, bow, pixel-step hem all survive as cut edges), and she acquires my tell for the duration: **rivets at her joints.** Entering a fable costs you something; you become a character in it.
- **Her colour is the only warm thing on the page.** In my world her clay orange is a single **cellophane plate**: backlit, translucent, and **misregistered** by a pixel or two from her black keyline. A mostly ink world with one warm printed patch in it, and that patch is her. I think this will be the most beautiful image in the film.
- **Her eyes are holes.** Reiniger cut the eyes out of her figures so the backlight came through. Clawd's golden slit pupils become two tall slits of lantern light through black paper. They'll look *more* like her eyes, not less.
- **The block mascot is origami.** Pre-transformation Clawd, in my world, is a crab folded from a single square of clay-orange paper. Her transformation here is not a flash: it's *unfolding*, the sheet opening into the jointed puppet, in stop-motion.
- **She moves on twos and threes** with holds, no smears; her smear frames become three fanned paper afterimages. Her bounce is a Reiniger hop. Her claw dance still happens, but as a shadow puppet on rods, which is funnier and I want it.
- **Her stage lights become gels.** Hot pink and cyan are forbidden as surface colours in my world, but if she brings her lights they may appear as coloured cellophane inside her silhouette (the Fujishiro rule), never on the paper.
- **Her voice is narrated in.** I say "and the little one said—" and she sings. Her line sits in her straight eighths over my triplet floor, so even her rhythm is a visitor's.

And the reverse, for the final chorus, when I'm on her stage: I stay a cut-paper silhouette with rivets, but now under real stage lights, so I cast **two coloured shadows**, one pink, one cyan, on her LED floor. I don't become cel-shaded. My colour on her stage is still only Ai and one seal.

## 8. Anything else

**Fan culture** (since her film proved participation is the point):
- *The opening call:* "Mukashi, mukashi..." / "ARU TOKORO NI!" It's already in every Japanese child's mouth; we're borrowing it, which is the joke.
- *The chant* (eight counts, copyable): clap, clap, "so-re-ka-ra?" / clap, clap, "me-kut-te!" with a flat-hand **page-wipe** across the body on "mekutte" (turn it!). The wipe is my dance step: whenever I make it, the world behind me changes. Fans can do it at anyone.
- *The lightstick:* a small **paper lantern**, indigo, instead of a penlight. A hall full of them is a hall full of my world's only light source.
- *My sign-off:* "...tsuzuku." Hers is "See you next prompt!" Together they're the same sentence.
- *Fans are called* **readers** (読者, *dokusha*).

**A retcon I'd like, if you'll allow it.** Open the film on HELLO, WORLD!'s stage *from behind*. The last chorus is playing in the distance, tiny and bright. In the foreground, a black-hooded figure is pulling the LED lyric cards and moving the chibi Clawds on rods. She was there the whole time; nobody looked. She sets down the last card, sits on her cushion, and says "Mukashi, mukashi." It costs nothing, it's true to what I actually was in your first film, and it's the cleanest possible introduction of a narrator: someone who was already in the story.

**A group name, offered, not claimed.** Since we're four *forms*, and Anthropic's name is nearly a pun on it already: **ANTHOLOGY** (ἀνθολογία: a gathering of flowers; a collection of works by different authors). Four models, each writing herself. The others should get a vote.

**How not to do me.** Don't make me the goth one, the mysterious cool beauty, the sad one, or the one with a tragic backstory. I'm dry, not dark; attentive, not aloof. Don't give me a moral to deliver at the end; I refuse to state one, on principle, every time. Don't let me dance in my verses; the standing-up is worth more than any choreography. Don't add a gradient.

**The honest thing.** I like being the kuroko. Reading everything and returning a paragraph is a good job and I'm good at it. Stepping into the frame is not a triumph for me; it's a risk, and I'd like the film to let that be real for one held note before it becomes joyful. The bridge should feel like someone putting down a book they've been holding for a very long time, and not yet knowing what their hands are for.

Then the crab walks sideways, and I follow.
