**Top 5 Problems (Ranked by severity)**

**1. 0:00.0 - 0:08.0 | The Hook is Dead on Arrival**
**The Problem:** You waste the first 8 seconds of a social video on a ticking counter and an almost flat orange line. In the social-first space, this is a fatal error; 90% of the audience will swipe away before the landscape ever appears. The pacing is built for a gallery installation, not a feed.
**The Fix:** Start the landscape generation immediately. The mountains, sun, and music track need to begin building from frame zero. You can still explain the concept of *t* over the first few seconds, but the visual reward must be present instantly.

**2. 0:24.0 - 0:30.0 | The Code Block is Mobile Garbage**
**The Problem:** You drop a massive block of unformatted, low-contrast text on screen at the climax. On a phone screen, this is completely illegible. You are asking the viewer to read raw syntax while the animation has essentially stopped, killing the momentum of the piece.
**The Fix:** Do not show the whole script. Crop tightly to the specific 3-4 lines that actually dictate the "drawing the whole past again" logic. Use basic syntax highlighting (different colors for variables/functions) to make it visually digestible, and scale it up 300% so it's actually readable on a 6-inch screen. 

**3. 0:00.0 - 0:24.0 | Mismanaged Screen Real Estate**
**The Problem:** Your visual hierarchy is upside down. The most visually boring element—the running counter `t = X.XXX`—dominates the top half of the screen at a massive point size. The hero element—the generated landscape and the tracing line—is squashed into the bottom third. 
**The Fix:** Shrink the counter by 60% and anchor it to the top left or right corner as a persistent HUD element. Scale the landscape up to occupy the lower two-thirds of the frame, giving the halftone textures and wave generation room to breathe.

**4. 0:08.0 | The Lazy Reveal**
**The Problem:** The transition from the empty line to the full landscape and sun is a hard, unmotivated pop. You spend the whole video talking about how the system "draws the whole past again," but you don't actually animate the drawing of the landscape—it just snaps into existence. It contradicts the script.
**The Fix:** Animate the landscape wiping or drawing on from left to right, matching the speed and position of the leading edge of the orange line as it hits that 8-second mark. Make the visuals prove the concept you are explaining.

**5. 0:01.0 - 0:23.0 | Typographic Disconnect**
**The Problem:** Mixing a heavy, monospaced digital font for the math with a delicate, traditional serif italic for the narration feels accidental rather than designed. Furthermore, the italic text jumps around the screen (center, left-aligned, right-aligned) without a clear grid system.
**The Fix:** Pick a typographic lane. If you want the contrast between "code" and "poetry," keep the fonts, but lock the narrative text to a rigid, invisible grid (e.g., always centered in the upper third). Stop letting the text float randomly; treat it with the same structural logic as the code you are referencing.

**Two Things That Work (Do not change these)**

*   **The Textural Aesthetic:** The risograph-style halftone patterns on the sun and mountains, combined with the subtle paper texture background, work very well. It provides much-needed warmth to a cold, code-based concept. 
*   **The Tracing Metaphor:** The visual of the orange line navigating the peaks and valleys as a representation of "the song so far" is a strong conceptual anchor. Once the landscape is actually visible, that specific motion graphic does its job perfectly.
