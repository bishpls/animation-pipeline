Here are the notes on this piece.

1. **0:13.5** - The narrative text ("these hills are...") gets swallowed by the rising blue graphics. It is a fundamental error to let secondary moving elements destroy the legibility of your primary copy.
**Fix:** Anchor all narrative text in the top third of the frame, permanently establishing a safe zone well above the maximum amplitude of the wave graphics.

2. **0:24.0** - The sudden appearance of the code block is a compositional failure. It abruptly covers the sun—the focal point you just spent 20 seconds building—and presents far too much text to read in the remaining six seconds.
**Fix:** Cut the code block entirely; the preceding text already explained the metaphor. If it must stay, treat it as a subtle, low-opacity background texture that fades in much earlier, not as a dominant foreground element that destroys the layout.

3. **0:30.0** - The piece doesn't end; it just stops. The hard cut on the visual freeze and the audio chop is jarring and lacks polish for a standalone social asset.
**Fix:** Add a 1-to-2-second decay. Fade the audio out smoothly and dissolve the graphics back to the base paper texture to provide a proper, deliberate resolution.

4. **0:16.5** - The typographic layout is haphazard. The shift from the positioning of the early text to the sudden center-ish alignment of "I write like this too:" feels accidental and breaks the viewer's reading flow.
**Fix:** Establish and stick to a rigid typographic grid. Decide on an alignment (left or center) and a specific screen position for all narrative phrases so the viewer knows exactly where to look when reading.

5. **0:08.5** - The `t = ...` counter is necessary for the initial hook, but once the landscape builds, it becomes dead weight. It dominates the top-center of the frame and competes unnecessarily with the narrative text below it.
**Fix:** Animate the counter scaling down and moving to a discreet corner (like top right) right around 0:08 when the sun and textures appear. Demote it to a secondary UI element once its primary job is done.

**Two things that work and must be kept:**

1. The visual payoff of the single orange line generating the layered, halftone landscape. It’s a clean, satisfying realization of the core concept.
2. The tight, frame-accurate sync between the audio transients and the initial pulsing movement of the lead orange line. It establishes immediate trust that the motion design is deliberate.
