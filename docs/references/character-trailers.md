## The conventions

1. **Open on Melee's real challenger screen**, 5–6 s, and play with its silhouette: none for Tails, a misdirecting star for Dedede.
2. **A staged story comes first, 40–55 s of it,** set in a recognisable Melee context (Classic mode's metal battle; the How to Play movie) and led by an existing hero, Sonic or Mario. It escalates, then a **setback**: Sonic downed, Mario flattened. Long holds for drama (a 10 s shot of the fallen hero; 6 s of Mario looking around).
3. **The reveal is by parts:** feet, tails, a hammer, an emblem, a belly, in extreme close-up, and the face last. The name card follows at once, **at 40–45% of the runtime**.
4. **Reaction shots and character interaction:** two-shots, grins, winks, a split-screen face-off. The newcomer is introduced through other characters' eyes.
5. **The montage never shows a move in a void.** Every shot is a situation: a different stage nearly every time, and always an opponent, an item, an enemy or a hazard. The moveset comes across through what happens to people. About 20 stages in each trailer.
6. **Impact frames** of 4–8 frames on big hits, and full-frame flashes on clashes.
7. **The new stage gets its own reveal** (Mt. Dedede, ~28 s): details first, then the wide, then a source-game reference staged on it (Kirby Super Star's boss card), then a fight.
8. **The end card is the game's own UI:** Melee's message box, with a line in the game's voice and the version and date.
9. **Length:** 1:46 and 2:15. The montage is only ~35 s of each; the rest is story and spectacle.

## What it meant for Geno's trailer (an example of applying it)

- **No move demos.** Every move shot in treatment v2.1 has to become a situation, or go.
- **The vignette is the trailer's spine,** and it needs a story with a setback: SMRPG's own opening gives us one (Mario storms the castle, the sky splits, everyone flies, a star falls).
- **Reveal Geno by parts,** face last, then the card. The star-shaped challenger silhouette is ours by right: Geno *is* the star.
- **The montage moves through many stages,** each shot against someone, with the SMRPG gags as situations, not inserts.
- **The Forest Maze gets a Mt. Dedede-style reveal,** with the SMRPG battle staged on it as its source-game moment.
- **End on Melee's own UI:** the prize screen in Geno's voice.
